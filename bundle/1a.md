The Zettelkasten method was developed by German sociologist Niklas Luhmann starting in the 1950s.
He used it to write over 70 books and 550 articles.
The system also inspired hypertext applications and the invention of wikis.
The core principle is one idea per note card.
Each card receives a unique hierarchical address like 21/3a.
This numbering allows new cards to be inserted anywhere in the sequence.
Cards link to each other through cross-references at the bottom.
An alphabetized index of keywords maps topics to card addresses.
Browsing through physically filed cards creates structured accidents.
These accidental juxtapositions generate insights a search would never reveal.
The system becomes a communication partner or second mind over time.
It develops thoughts rather than merely storing information.
