A genetic algorithm is a metaheuristic inspired by natural selection belonging to the class of evolutionary algorithms.
It evolves a population of candidate solutions toward better solutions across successive generations.
Each candidate solution has a chromosome representation that can be mutated and altered.
The evolution starts from a randomly generated population.
In each generation the fitness of every individual is evaluated using an objective function.
Fitter individuals are selected from the current population.
Their chromosomes are recombined through crossover and randomly mutated to form the next generation.
The process repeats until a termination condition is met.
Common termination conditions include a maximum number of generations or a satisfactory fitness level.
The building block hypothesis suggests GAs implicitly identify and recombine short high-fitness schemata to construct better solutions.
Genetic algorithms have practical limitations.
Fitness evaluation for complex problems is often computationally expensive.
GAs can converge to local optima rather than the global optimum.
